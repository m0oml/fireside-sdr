# Fireside SDR: short user guide

Fireside SDR receives and **transmits**. It only transmits when you press a transmit button (Tune, Measure drive, Test tone, Send, Repeat, CQ, or the voice PTT). Another program (WSJT-X and the like) can key it over CAT only while you have ticked **Let other programs key** in this window (see "Using WSJT-X and other programs"). Radio writes (start-up, tuning) are off until you tick **Allow writes** and confirm; that is never remembered. **You are responsible for your licence, your antenna and your signal.** Read "Transmitting" below before your first over, and see the warning in the top-level README.

## Starting
- Launcher: **Fireside SDR** (install with `system/install-launcher.sh`; its right-click menu has **Simulated radio (no hardware)**).
- Terminal: `build/gui/fireside --simulate` runs against a simulated radio; `build/gui/fireside` is the real one.
- No radio? **Open file...** plays any I/Q WAV (recordings go in `recordings/`).

## Radio screen
- **Tune:** click the spectrum, mouse wheel (Shift = 10x), Left/Right keys, PgUp/PgDn for band. Space = play/pause, M = mute.
- **Mode and filter:** USB, LSB, CW, AM, FM. Low and High cut are separate; drag the shaded edges on the spectrum, Ctrl+drag inside to slide the whole passband. Skirt: Soft / Normal / Sharp.
- **Noise reduction:** the Noise card has **NR2** for voice (SSB, AM, FM): it learns the noise and turns it down. Tested on speech in real band noise it improved the signal-to-noise ratio of the audio by about 5 dB (the older NR made it 2 to 3 dB worse on speech). It adds about 0.1 s of delay, is not used for CW, and switches itself off while PSK31, RTTY, FT8 or FT4 are decoding (they work better on the untouched audio). **NR** is the older line filter: it keeps steady tones, so use it on CW or whistles, not speech. NR2 needs the system library `libfftw3` (package `libfftw3-3`); without it the button is missing.
- **Notches:** Alt+click the spectrum to add one (up to 4), Alt+click one to remove, drag to move. Works in SSB and CW.
- **S-meter:** S1 to S9+60 with a dBm number. Once the radio has started and the transmit check has read the EEPROM, the dBm comes from the radio's own factory **RX level table** (per band; the preamp's gain is taken off while the preamp is on) and is marked *(factory table)*. Before that, or outside the amateur bands, it is a placeholder marked *(uncal)*. The factory table has not yet been checked against a known signal here.
- **Band plan** (Waterfall card): coloured strip on the spectrum (green CW, violet digital, blue phone, amber beacons) and FT8/FT4 calling frequencies. IARU Region 1, a guide only.
- **Decoder:** CW, PSK31, RTTY, FT8, FT4. For FT8/FT4, double-click a line to fill the Lookup box. Lines are tagged **NEW**, **new band/mode** or **worked** from your Wavelog. A line calling you is highlighted red and the caller is looked up by itself (the first caller; a call you typed is left alone). With your QRZ.com login on the Log tab (QRZ.com callbook card), each station's name, town, country and distance appear after its line.
- **Voice / CW / Digi switch** (left of the frequency): chooses what the screen shows. **Voice**: USB, LSB, AM, FM and the voice transmit strip (no decoder). **CW**: the CW button and the Morse decoder. **Digi**: FT8, FT4, PSK31, RTTY, the decoder and the message card. Clicking a group also sets a sensible mode (Voice turns the decoder off; Digi goes back to your last digital mode, FT8 first, on its calling frequency). The red **TX** light above the switch is lit while the radio is keyed. The passband (filter) controls are in the Noise and filter card.
- **Band memory:** each band button (and PgUp/PgDn) takes you back to where you left that band: frequency, mode, filter and decoder (FT8 on 20 m stays FT8 on 20 m). A band you have not used yet starts at its usual frequency, or at its FT8/FT4 frequency if that decoder is on. Remembered between runs. The antenna per band is set on the Antennas tab.
- **CAT for other programs** (Device tab, on by default): WSJT-X, JTDX, fldigi, GridTracker, WaveLogGate and other Hamlib programs can follow and set the frequency and mode. In the other program choose the rig **Hamlib NET rigctl**, address `127.0.0.1:4532`. Only this computer can connect. Its transmit requests are refused (and counted on the card) unless you allow them: see below.

## Transmitting
Receive never depends on any of this. Transmit needs a radio that has been started with **Allow writes**, and a suitable antenna on **ANT1** (the ATU's port; transmit stays on ANT1). Start with low power, preferably into a proper antenna or a dummy load.
- **The EEPROM backup (first time).** The radio's factory calibration lives in its EEPROM and the transmit setup reads it. When the radio starts, the app reads it (read-only, about 16 s), compares it byte for byte with the saved copy in `~/.local/share/fireside/backups`, and sets the power amplifier's bias from it. **The first time there is no copy, so the app asks "Save this radio's calibration?"** Say yes: it reads the EEPROM twice (about half a minute), keeps the copy as a read-only file that is never overwritten, and then allows transmit. Keep your own second copy elsewhere. The app never writes the EEPROM. If the radio's EEPROM ever differs from your copy, transmit is refused and the message says how many bytes differ; **Retry transmit check** appears if the check fails.
- **Tuner** (Antenna and tuner card, top right): **Tune ATU**, and **Tuner settings...** which opens a small window with the tune power (default 3 W), the SWR threshold, **Restore ATU**, the ATU memory line and a **Calibration** fold (**Measure drive**, **Test tone**). In the tuner, the result is remembered per band and **Restore ATU** puts it back without any RF. **Stop** unkeys at once (on the Digi message card, the voice strip and the Tuner settings window). **Measure drive (2 W)** is a short ramp that measures your radio's drive level for the band; do it once per band before Tune works there: there is no built-in drive level, because one measured on a different radio is not trusted. **Test tone (2 W, 8 s)** sends a steady tone 1 kHz above the dial so you can find it on another receiver.
- **Tune and Measure drive ask first ("listen first")**: they put a carrier on the air, so make sure the frequency is clear. Test tone, Send and Repeat do not ask.
- **Meters:** the Transmit power meter in the same tile as the Signal meter shows forward and reflected watts and SWR while keyed, and the last over afterwards. The **TX** lamp is red while keyed. The SWR is read from the radio's own power detector, which over-reads at low mismatch (about 1.5 to 2 where the tuner reports none); treat it as a backstop for gross faults.
- **Safety limits.** There is a time limit on every key-down, a forward-power and SWR trip, and a hard cap on the transmit amplitude; the radio is unkeyed on any error. If the unkey command ever fails, the card says **UNKEY FAILED** and a dialog tells you to power the radio off at once.
- **FT8 / FT4:** type a message (or press **CQ**, which needs your call and locator set; it never guesses), pick even or odd and a frequency, then **Send**; **Repeat** calls every slot of that parity until **Stop**. Receive pauses about 0.8 s before your over and comes back afterwards. The app trims its drive to the power you set during the first 3 s of an over.
- **Click to reply + auto sequence + auto log** (on by default): double-click a CQ, or a station calling you, and the app sets the slot and the right message, then answers each step (report, R-report, RR73, 73) in your slots, repeats a message up to 6 times (CQ up to 12), and logs the QSO to Wavelog once the completing over has gone out. **Stop** ends it.
- **QSO pane and callers area.** The decoder is split: on the left every decode; on the right the **QSO pane** with only what is addressed to your call (red) and your own overs (amber **TX**), always scrolled to the newest line. The top four lines of the decoded text are the **callers area**: everyone who has called you stays there, fading, until you answer them (they then move to the QSO pane) or they go unanswered for 6 FT8 or 10 FT4 cycles. A double-click on either replies.
- **Voice (SSB):** PEP 1 to 20 W, PTT by holding the button, the space bar (if ticked) or the mic or rear PTT. It is tested on the simulated radio but **not yet used on the air**: treat it as untested.
- **PSK Reporter:** a tab shows where you are heard; uploading your decodes is off by default (tick box).

## Using WSJT-X and other programs
Fireside SDR can be the radio for WSJT-X, JTDX, JS8Call, fldigi and other programs: they get the receive audio, send their transmit audio, follow and set the frequency, and key the radio.
- **Once:** run `system/setup-system.sh`. Among other things it adds two virtual sound devices, **Fireside RX audio** and **Fireside TX audio** (a PipeWire file in `~/.config/pipewire`; `--remove` takes it out again).
- **In the other program:**
  - Radio: **Hamlib NET rigctl**, `127.0.0.1:4532`, PTT method **CAT**, Split **None**.
  - Audio input: **Monitor of Fireside RX audio**. Audio output: **Fireside TX audio**.
  - If the start of each over sounds clipped, give the program a short transmit delay (about 0.3 s).
- **In Fireside SDR:**
  - Tick **External program** on the decoder card. Our decoder, auto sequence + log and PSK Reporter uploads turn off, so nothing is decoded, logged or spotted twice, and the receive audio goes to Fireside RX audio. Untick it and they come back as they were.
  - Use the direct stream (48 / 96 / 192 on the Radio screen), so receive keeps running while the other program transmits.
  - Measure drive once on the band (Transmit card), as for our own overs.
  - Tick **Let other programs key** on the **Audio** tab (Other programs card) and set the power beside it. **This is off in every new window**: you tick it each session.
- **What a keyed over does:** the other program's audio is sent as it is (no processing). The power beside the tick is reached when its audio is at full scale; quieter audio gives less, never more.
- **Limits:** at most 2 minutes per over; the radio unkeys if the program releases PTT, stops talking to us for 15 s, disconnects, or you untick the box, press Stop or stop the radio. Only one program can key at a time, only in the amateur bands, and the dial cannot be moved while keyed. The forward-power, SWR and time limits of every over apply.
- **What is still ours:** the Transmit power meter and TX lamp show every over, and **Stop** always unkeys. Start at low power and watch the meter on the first overs.
- This route has been tested on the simulated radio and with real Hamlib `rigctl`, not yet on the air with WSJT-X.

## Logging (Wavelog)
- Set up once on the **Log** tab: server URL, API v2 token, **Test connection**. The token is kept in `~/.config/fireside/wavelog.env` (mode 600), never in the app's settings.
- **Lookup box:** type a callsign, Enter = who is it and have I worked them. Enter again (or **Log**) opens the log form; Enter in a field logs; Esc cancels.
- Every QSO is saved locally first (`~/.local/share/fireside/log.adi`). If Wavelog is unreachable it is kept in `unsent.adi`; press **Send unsent** later.
- **Last contacts** shows the newest 12 from Wavelog (or from this PC when offline).
