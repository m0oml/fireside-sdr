# Fireside SDR: short user guide

Fireside SDR receives and **transmits**. It only transmits when you press a transmit button (Tune, Measure drive, Test tone, Send, CQ (with or without Attended CQ), or the voice PTT). Another program (WSJT-X and the like) can key it over CAT only while you have ticked **Let other programs key** in this window (see "Using WSJT-X and other programs"). Radio writes (start-up, tuning) are off until you tick **Allow writes** and confirm; that is never remembered. **You are responsible for your licence, your antenna and your signal.** Read "Transmitting" below before your first over, and see the warning in the top-level README.

## Starting
- Launcher: **Fireside SDR** (install with `system/install-launcher.sh`; its right-click menu has **Simulated radio (no hardware)**).
- Terminal: `build/gui/fireside --simulate` runs against a simulated radio; `build/gui/fireside` is the real one.
- No radio? **Open file...** plays any I/Q WAV (recordings go in `recordings/`).

## Radio screen
- **Tune:** click the spectrum, mouse wheel (Shift = 10x), Left/Right keys, PgUp/PgDn for band. Space = play/pause, M = mute.
- **Mode and filter:** USB, LSB, CW, AM, FM. Low and High cut are separate; drag the shaded edges on the spectrum, Ctrl+drag inside to slide the whole passband. Skirt: Soft / Normal / Sharp.
- **Noise reduction:** the Noise card has **NR2** for voice (SSB, AM, FM): it learns the noise and turns it down. Tested on speech in real band noise it improved the signal-to-noise ratio of the audio by about 5 dB (the older NR made it 2 to 3 dB worse on speech). It adds about 0.1 s of delay, is not used for CW, and switches itself off while PSK31, RTTY, FT8 or FT4 are decoding (they work better on the untouched audio). **NR** is the older line filter: it keeps steady tones, so use it on CW or whistles, not speech. NR2 needs the system library `libfftw3` (package `libfftw3-3`); without it the button is missing.
- **Notches:** Alt+click the spectrum to add one (up to 4), Alt+click one to remove, drag to move. Works in SSB and CW.
- **S-meter:** S1 to S9+60 with a dBm number. Once the radio has started and the transmit check has read the EEPROM, the dBm comes from the radio's own factory **RX level table** (per band; the preamp's gain is taken off while the preamp is on) and is marked *(factory table)*. Before that, or outside the amateur bands, it is a placeholder marked *(uncal)*. **Check S-meter (internal generator)** (Tuner settings > Calibration, in USB or LSB) checks it on the current band: receive only, nothing is transmitted. For about 10 s the receiver listens to the radio's built-in signal generator instead of the antenna, taken as -24 dBm as PowerSDR does, with the preamp off and then on. The result is saved for that band and the meter shows *(checked)*. The message compares it with the factory table. If the radio ever stays on the generator (the check reports it could not switch it off), stop the radio and power-cycle it.
- **Band plan** (Waterfall card): coloured strip on the spectrum (green CW, violet digital, blue phone, amber beacons) and FT8/FT4 calling frequencies. IARU Region 1, a guide only.
- **Decoder:** CW, PSK31, RTTY, FT8, FT4. For FT8/FT4, double-click a line to fill the Lookup box. Lines are tagged **NEW**, **new band/mode** or **worked** from your Wavelog. Above those, from your whole log (all of it at start-up, then only new QSOs every 10 minutes, and updated as you log): **NEW DXCC** (magenta), **DXCC new band** (pink), **new continent** (violet), **new CQ zone** / **new ITU zone** (cyan) and **new grid** / **grid new band** (blue). Only the most valuable one shows. Choose them on the Log tab, **Highlight on decodes** card (zones are off by default: cty.dat gives one zone for all of the US, Canada, Russia and Australia, so they are only rough there). Countries come from the callsign (cty.dat, as WSJT-X does), grids from the locator the station sent. The tags only colour the lines: they do not change who auto mode answers. A line calling you is highlighted red and the caller is looked up by itself (the first caller; a call you typed is left alone). With your QRZ.com login on the Log tab (QRZ.com callbook card), each station's name, town, country and distance appear after its line.
- **Voice / CW / Digi switch** (left of the frequency): chooses what the screen shows. **Voice**: USB, LSB, AM, FM and the voice transmit strip (no decoder). **CW**: the CW button and the Morse decoder. **Digi**: FT8, FT4, PSK31, RTTY, the decoder and the message card. Clicking a group also sets a sensible mode (Voice turns the decoder off; Digi goes back to your last digital mode, FT8 first, on its calling frequency). The red **TX** light above the switch is lit while the radio is keyed. The passband (filter) controls are in the Noise and filter card.
- **Band memory:** each band button (and PgUp/PgDn) takes you back to where you left that band: frequency, mode, filter and decoder (FT8 on 20 m stays FT8 on 20 m). A band you have not used yet starts at its usual frequency, or at its FT8/FT4 frequency if that decoder is on. Remembered between runs. The antenna per band is set on the Antennas tab.
- **If the receive stream stops** ("no data from the radio"; a heavily loaded PC or a FireWire upset can do it), the app restarts just the stream by itself, as pressing 48 / 96 / 192 does, and says so in the status line. After 3 restarts within 2 minutes it stops trying: press **Stop radio**, then start it again. While the stream is stopped nothing can transmit: an over in progress stops, and a new one is refused.
- **CAT for other programs** (Device tab, on by default): WSJT-X, JTDX, fldigi, GridTracker, WaveLogGate and other Hamlib programs can follow and set the frequency and mode. In the other program choose the rig **Hamlib NET rigctl**, address `127.0.0.1:4532`. Only this computer can connect. Its transmit requests are refused (and counted on the card) unless you allow them: see below.

## Transmitting
Receive never depends on any of this. Transmit needs a radio that has been started with **Allow writes**, and a suitable antenna on **ANT1** (the ATU's port; transmit stays on ANT1). Start with low power, preferably into a proper antenna or a dummy load.
- **The EEPROM backup (first time).** The radio's factory calibration lives in its EEPROM and the transmit setup reads it. When the radio starts, the app reads it (read-only, about 16 s), compares it byte for byte with the saved copy in `~/.local/share/fireside/backups`, and sets the power amplifier's bias from it. **The first time there is no copy, so the app asks "Save this radio's calibration?"** Say yes: it reads the EEPROM twice (about half a minute), keeps the copy as a read-only file that is never overwritten, and then allows transmit. Keep your own second copy elsewhere. The app never writes the EEPROM. If the radio's EEPROM ever differs from your copy, transmit is refused and the message says how many bytes differ; **Retry transmit check** appears if the check fails.
- **Tuner** (Antenna and tuner card, top right): **Tune ATU**, and **Tuner settings...** which opens a small window with the tune power (default 3 W), the SWR threshold, **Restore ATU**, the ATU memory line and a **Calibration** fold (**Measure drive**, **Test tone**). In the tuner, the result is remembered per band and **Restore ATU** puts it back without any RF. **Stop** unkeys at once (on the Digi message card, the voice strip and the Tuner settings window). **Measure drive (2 W)** is a short ramp that measures your radio's drive level for the band; do it once per band before Tune works there: there is no built-in drive level, because one measured on a different radio is not trusted. **Test tone (2 W, 8 s)** sends a steady tone 1 kHz above the dial so you can find it on another receiver.
- **Tune and Measure drive ask first ("listen first")**: they put a carrier on the air, so make sure the frequency is clear. Test tone, Send, CQ and Attended CQ do not ask.
- **Meters:** the Transmit power meter in the same tile as the Signal meter shows forward and reflected watts and SWR while keyed, and the last over afterwards. The **TX** lamp is red while keyed. The SWR is read from the radio's own power detector, which over-reads at low mismatch (about 1.5 to 2 where the tuner reports none); treat it as a backstop for gross faults.
- **Safety limits.** There is a time limit on every key-down, a forward-power and SWR trip, and a hard cap on the transmit amplitude; the radio is unkeyed on any error. If the unkey command fails, the app keeps retrying it for 2 s and never keys on top of it; only if it still has not got through does the card say **UNKEY FAILED** and a dialog tell you to power the radio off at once. At 100 W the forward-power trip is 110 W (130 W at once). After any safety stop nothing transmits again by itself: not the next CQ, not the next waiting caller. A tuner "pass" that came only after the app's own time or power limit cut the carrier, or with an SWR above 3, is not saved.
- **FT8 / FT4:** type a message (or press **CQ**, which needs your call and locator set; it never guesses), pick even or odd and a frequency, then **Send**. **Attended CQ** (it was Repeat) keeps calling while you are at the radio, until **Stop**:
  - **CQ** goes out 6 times in your chosen slot, then nothing for 4 slots (1 minute in FT8, 30 s in FT4) while both slots are decoded, then 6 more CQs in the **other** slot (even and odd swap each time), and so on. **Clear freq** looks again before each block. A station answering during the pause is worked straight away, in the slot after theirs.
  - **A message you typed** is sent 6 times, then it goes back to CQ.
  - **With Auto sequence:** a QSO that ends, logged or given up, goes back to CQ (after any callers waiting), including one you started by double-clicking a station. Double-clicking leaves Attended CQ ticked.
  - **Stop**, a safety stop, losing receive or changing band ends it and unticks the box; tick it again to carry on. Without Attended CQ, a CQ with Auto sequence stops by itself after 12 calls.
   **Clear freq** (on by default): pressing **CQ** checks the stations decoded in the last 2 minutes (1 minute in FT4); if one is on or next to your transmit frequency, it moves to the middle of the widest clear gap between 300 and 2700 Hz and says so. A frequency that is already clear is kept. Replies stay on your frequency. Receive pauses about 0.8 s before your over and comes back afterwards. The app trims its drive to the power you set during the first 3 s of an over. **Power:** FT8/FT4, voice and other programs go up to 100 W (the radio's rating), default 20 W; a band's limit comes from its measured drive level (press Measure drive once per band). Tune, Measure drive and Test tone stay at 15 W or less. FT8 is a full carrier for about 13 s of every 30 s: around half the rating is usual for long sessions.
- **Click to reply + auto sequence + auto log** (on by default): double-click a CQ, or a station calling you, and the app sets the slot and the right message, then answers each step (report, R-report, RR73, 73) in your slots, repeats a message up to 6 times (CQ up to 12), and logs the QSO to Wavelog once the completing over has gone out. **Stop** ends it. So does changing band: the QSO or CQ, anything queued and the callers waiting on the old band are dropped. A station whose callsign has a prefix no country uses (such as 0J0BQR: almost always a false decode made from noise) is never answered by itself, not as an answer to your CQ and not from the callers list. A double-click on one asks first; double-click again within 15 s to answer anyway. The decoder also drops weak and a-priori ("a1", "a2") decodes with such a callsign. If the receive stream stops by itself (RF getting into the shack, a FireWire bus reset), automatic transmit stops at once: an over on the air unkeys, the QSO or CQ ends and nothing stays queued, and a red box says why. It stays off even when the stream comes back by itself, until you press CQ or Send, or double-click a station. A tune or your own voice PTT is not stopped.
- **Status bar** (along the bottom of the window):
  - **False decodes dropped:** how many weak or a-priori decodes the decoder has thrown away since the app started, because a callsign has a prefix no country uses or the locator is impossible for it. Hover over it to see the last one. When auto mode or a double-click holds back for such a station, the reason shows on the left for 20 s.
  - **Supply:** the radio's 13.8 V supply, as the radio measures it on the PA board (read-only, as PowerSDR's ZZRV). On transmit it shows the lowest reading during the over, and afterwards "last TX" keeps it. A big drop on transmit points at the power supply or the DC leads. Amber below 13.0 V; red below 12.5 V or at 15 V and above.
  - **PA:** the PA temperature sensor, read on receive. Amber from 70 °C, red from 100 °C.
- **QSO pane and callers area.** The decoder is split: on the left every decode; on the right the **QSO pane** with only what is addressed to your call (red) and your own overs (amber **TX**), always scrolled to the newest line. The top four lines of the decoded text are the **callers area**: everyone who has called you stays there, with no time limit, until you work them or move on (the newest three and a count are shown when more than four wait). In auto mode, when a QSO ends the newest waiting caller is answered, with 3 tries before moving on to the next; a station worked or given up on leaves the list and is not listed again for 10 minutes. A double-click on either replies.
- **Voice (SSB):** PEP 1 to 100 W (default 20), PTT by holding the button, the space bar (if ticked) or the mic or rear PTT. It is tested on the simulated radio but **not yet used on the air**: treat it as untested.
  - **Feedback test (no RF):** Audio tab, **Test input** with **Hear it processed** ticked. Your voice goes through the same processing as an over (EQ, leveler, compressor, SSB filter) and plays on the PC as a receiver would hear it, with the input and processed levels beside it. Use headphones (speakers howl back into the mic). A change to the processing is heard within half a second. 0 dBFS processed is the set power (PEP), and power goes with the square of the level: -3 dBFS is half, -6 dBFS a quarter. Aim for about -1 to -2 dBFS on normal speech; the leveler target (default -6 dBFS) is the main control for it.
  - **VOX:** tick **VOX** under the voice controls (Voice screen, transmit ready). Speech above the threshold keys an over; it unkeys after the hang (default 800 ms) when you stop. The first 150 ms are kept from before the key, so the first syllable is sent (the over runs 150 ms behind your voice). The level beside it shows the mic as VOX hears it: set the threshold above your room noise. **Anti-VOX** stops the speaker's receive audio keying it (not needed with headphones). VOX is off in every new window, never remembered, and switches itself off after an over ends on its time-out (3 min) or a safety stop, or if an over is refused.
  - **Monitor:** hear your processed audio on the PC while you transmit (headphones).
  - Receive audio is off while the transmitter is keyed (any mode), so the speaker cannot feed back into the mic.
- **CW** (the CW screen, the **Send CW** card beside the Morse decoder). Tested on the simulated radio only: **not yet used on the air**.
  - **Paddle or key** on the radio's KEY jack (1/4" stereo plug: dot = tip, dash = ring, common = sleeve). With **Paddle keys** ticked, transmit ready and the screen on CW, pressing it starts an over by itself (semi break-in). The two lights show what the radio reports, so you can check the wiring with nothing transmitted.
  - **Keyer:** Iambic B or A (5 to 60 wpm, weight, Reverse to swap the paddles), or **Straight key**: either contact keys directly (also for an external keyer).
  - **Break-in delay** (default 600 ms): the transmitter stays keyed this long after the last element, then receive is back. The first element waits until the transmitter is keyed, a few milliseconds; it is never shortened.
  - **Text:** type it (or pick a memory from the TX lab) and press Send or Enter. Touching the paddle stops it.
  - **Power** 1 to 100 W (default 10). Needs the band's measured drive level (as voice). Limits as for the other modes: forward power, SWR 3, the 10-minute over, and a key held down for 15 s (a stuck key or a shorted lead) unkeys.
  - The carrier is exactly on the dial, where the receiver's CW mode listens (as PowerSDR does in CWU: the transmit oscillator sits 1 kHz below the dial for the over). Needs the direct FireWire stream (48 / 96 / 192). **Sidetone** plays on the PC's speakers; receive audio is off while keyed.
  - Check the KEY jack without transmitting: close the app, then `build/tools/firesidekey --fw /dev/fwN --backup TRX.bin key-test` prints each paddle and PTT change as the radio reports it, and how long each read takes. If the EVENT lines stay silent while the READ_PTT lines follow the paddle, put `key_source=read_ptt` under `[General]` in `~/.config/fireside/cw.conf` (with the app closed) and the app uses the slower command.
- **PSK Reporter:** a tab shows where you are heard; uploading your decodes is off by default (tick box).

## Rear TX outputs (band switching)

The radio's three rear RCA outputs, **TX1 (red)**, **TX2 (white)** and **TX3 (yellow)**, are open-collector switches to ground (100 V, 500 mA). On the **Antennas** tab, the **Rear TX outputs** card gives each one a role:

- **Unused** (the default): Fireside never touches the line.
- **Band switch:** a **TX1/TX2/TX3** tick column appears in the band table. The line is **on while the dial is in a ticked band**, on receive and transmit alike, for example a relay that turns the doublet into an 80 m T antenna.
- **Amplifier keying** and **Transverter:** placeholders, not built yet (shown, not selectable).

The rules are the same for every way of transmitting (Tune, FT8/FT4, CW, voice, CAT):
- A line changes only while the radio is running, and **never during an over** (the dial is locked then anyway).
- Before each key-down, Fireside sets the lines for that frequency and waits the **Relay settle time** (default 50 ms) after any change, so the relay never switches with RF on its contacts.
- If a line cannot be set (a mailbox fault), **nothing transmits** until you stop and start the radio again.
- The first time a line is used after the radio starts, Fireside tells the radio **not** to make it follow transmit (PowerSDR's default is to follow transmit, which would make a band relay click with every over).
- **Stop radio** and closing Fireside switch every used line **off**. Build the external gear so that **off is its safe position**.

Nothing here has been tried on the real radio yet: the first check is with a meter or an LED on the jacks, with no RF.

## Using WSJT-X and other programs
Fireside SDR can be the radio for WSJT-X, JTDX, JS8Call, fldigi and other programs: they get the receive audio, send their transmit audio, follow and set the frequency, and key the radio.
- **Once:** run `system/setup-system.sh`. Among other things it adds two virtual sound devices, **Fireside RX audio** and **Fireside TX audio** (a PipeWire file in `~/.config/pipewire`; `--remove` takes it out again).
- **In the other program:**
  - Radio: **Hamlib NET rigctl**, `127.0.0.1:4532`, PTT method **CAT**, Split **None**.
  - Audio input: **Monitor of Fireside RX audio**. Audio output: **Fireside TX audio**.
  - If the start of each over sounds clipped, give the program a short transmit delay (about 0.3 s).
- **In Fireside SDR:**
  - Tick **External program** on the decoder card. Our decoder, auto sequence + log and PSK Reporter uploads turn off, so nothing is decoded, logged or spotted twice, and the receive audio goes to Fireside RX audio. Untick it and they come back as they were.
  - Use the direct stream (48 / 96 / 192 on the Radio screen), so receive keeps running while the other program transmits.
  - Measure drive once on the band (Transmit card), as for our own overs.
  - Tick **Let other programs key** on the **Audio** tab (Other programs card) and set the power beside it. **This is off in every new window**: you tick it each session.
- **What a keyed over does:** the other program's audio is sent as it is (no processing). The power beside the tick is reached when its audio is at full scale; quieter audio gives less, never more.
- **Limits:** at most 2 minutes per over; the radio unkeys if the program releases PTT, stops talking to us for 15 s, disconnects, or you untick the box, press Stop or stop the radio. Only one program can key at a time, only in the amateur bands, and the dial cannot be moved while keyed. The forward-power, SWR and time limits of every over apply.
- **What is still ours:** the Transmit power meter and TX lamp show every over, and **Stop** always unkeys. Start at low power and watch the meter on the first overs.
- This route has been tested on the simulated radio and with real Hamlib `rigctl`, not yet on the air with WSJT-X.

## Logging (Wavelog)
- Set up once on the **Log** tab: server URL, API v2 token, **Test connection**. The token is kept in `~/.config/fireside/wavelog.env` (mode 600), never in the app's settings.
- **Lookup box:** type a callsign, Enter = who is it and have I worked them. Enter again (or **Log**) opens the log form; Enter in a field logs; Esc cancels.
- Every QSO is saved locally first (`~/.local/share/fireside/log.adi`). If Wavelog is unreachable it is kept in `unsent.adi`; press **Send unsent** later.
- **Last contacts** shows the newest 12 from Wavelog (or from this PC when offline).
- **Logging voice (or anything) in Wavelog's own page:** tick **Send frequency, mode and power to Wavelog** (Log tab, Follow the dial card; remembered). In Wavelog's live QSO page pick the radio named on the card (default `FLEX-5000A`): band, frequency, mode and power then fill in by themselves.
  - It sends on every change, and every 5 minutes otherwise, so Wavelog keeps the radio as current.
  - Mode is SSB for USB/LSB; digital modes go by name (FT8, FT4, PSK31, RTTY).
  - Power is the forward power measured on your last over, in whole watts. It is left out when you have not transmitted in the last 10 minutes.
  - The line under the tick shows the last thing sent, or Wavelog's error. After an error it tries again a minute later. A 403 means the token lacks the `radio:write` scope: make a new token with it in Wavelog.
